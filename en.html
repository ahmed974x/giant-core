Understand what moves markets, with evidence, not claims.

NABD is a research and learning platform built on public, open data, with five AI agents that read the physical world and check every number, plus a practice account in made-up money. We publish what we test, including what failed.

What works today

This is what actually exists. Nothing here connects to a real trading account.

Working

Practice account

Practise with a made-up balance. Every order is priced at the live public price plus a 0.1% fee and 0.05% slippage, so the result stays close to reality.

  • It never touches an exchange's trading API, holds no exchange keys and moves no money.
  • Every number is rebuilt from an event log, so it can be audited.
Working

Strategy lab

Five classic strategy types: trend, mean reversion, breakout, momentum and news. Each is tested on real historical candles with the same fee and slippage.

  • Results describe the past, not a promise about the future.
  • The most it can do is a trial run on the practice account.
Working

Deep Research

Ask in Arabic or English. It searches free open sources, reads the pages, writes an answer with a source link for each claim, and checks whether the source actually supports it.

Working

Newton, the maths engine

Exact distances, arrival times and equations. Every answer shows its inputs, formula, assumptions and evidence links, so you can check it yourself.

Data comes only from public, free or open-licensed sources, used within their terms.

Five agents, each with one clear job

Every agent records what it did and why, and passes through check gates before its output touches any number. The status next to each agent is its real state today.

Partly working

Quant maths agent

Works out probabilities and risk before any decision: Monte Carlo simulation, Bayesian inference, the Kelly criterion for position size, and realised volatility.

  • Every number is computed two independent ways and used only when both agree.
  • Runs today through Newton and the strategy lab, on the practice account only.
Partly working

Open-source intelligence agent

Reads the physical world from public sources: ships, flights, disasters, world events and open road cameras, and links them to commodities and currencies as hypotheses that are tested before use.

  • Every point is clickable, so you see the raw evidence yourself.
  • Automated image analysis (OpenCV / YOLO) is planned and not built yet.
Locked · needs explicit approval

Execution agent

Designed to place trades through open-source libraries, with a circuit breaker that stops everything on any fault and reconnects by itself.

  • Today it executes on the practice account only. No exchange keys, no real money.
  • Any real trading needs a separate, explicit approval from the account owner.
Design

Daily audit and planning agent

At the end of each day it reviews every practice-account trade, rebuilds the numbers from the event log, records mistakes, and drafts a plan for tomorrow.

  • It only proposes. Any strategy change goes through your approval.
Closed · legal review

Portfolio and profit-split agent

Designed to manage accounts and work out performance fees with full mathematical transparency, charged only above the previous high.

  • Not open to the public. We accept no money and no subscriptions.

How we read the physical world

Markets react to what happens in ports, shipping lanes, the sky and the roads. We collect these signals from free, open sources, link them to the assets they affect, and test whether they actually lead the market before we trust them.

Sea and sky

Public ship positions, flight traffic, satellites, and NASA satellite fire detections.

  • Digitraffic AIS
  • AISStream
  • OpenSky
  • NASA FIRMS

Events and disasters

Earthquakes, cyclones, humanitarian alerts and the global event database, each event linked to the assets it affects.

  • GDELT
  • USGS
  • GDACS
  • NOAA

Open cameras

Still images that government transport agencies publish for anyone, shown as they are with a link to their source. No private feeds and no bypassing of any protection.

  • TfL JamCams
  • Fintraffic
  • Vegagerðin

The chain: three finance research agents and one notebook

A researcher, then a model builder, then an earnings reviewer, and everything they find goes into one notes.md per stock. Research tools only: they never trade and never recommend.

Working for research

Market researcher

The last 90 days of news from free GDELT, sorted into company, regulatory and sector, plus a 6-month analyst range, bulls versus bears, and how management’s wording changed.

  • Every claim carries a source and a date; anything without one is marked “unverified”.
  • Machine readings are tagged [INFERENCE], and an information-gaps list is built automatically.
Working for research

Institutional model builder

Five sheets: INPUTS, HISTORICALS, PROJECTION, VALUATION (DCF and comps) and SENSITIVITY with a two-way matrix.

  • No number is typed into a formula; every assumption lives in INPUTS.
  • Shows the top 3 assumptions, the level that moves the price 30%, and the top 3 risks; exports CSV and Excel.
Working for research

Earnings reviewer

What was said against expectations, how it was said against last quarter, and which analyst questions were answered or dodged.

  • Tests your 3-sentence thesis against exact quotes from the call.
  • Asks for measurable falsification criteria before you rely on the thesis.
Working

The notes.md notebook

One growing file per stock: your view in 3 sentences, what could prove you wrong, the last review, and what changed since.

  • Each new review records how your view changed, automatically.
  • It stays in your browser, and you can download it as Markdown at any time.

Every number is tested, even when the answer is failure

Question: do physics-inspired indicators (Hurst, Lyapunov and a Reynolds-style ratio) warn of crises better than plain 20-day volatility? Answer: no.

Score is ROC AUC: 0.5 = a coin flip, higher is better. Target: the worst 10% of drawdowns over the next 20 trading days. Brackets: 95% confidence interval.

Result: failed. None of the three indicators beat plain volatility. The well-known thresholds (Reynolds above 2000 or 4000) were never reached on any asset, and Lyapunov above zero held on 100% of days, even on shuffled random data, so it carries no information.

The best result was Reynolds* on Bitcoin, so we gave it one last test on 2023–2026 data with a pass rule fixed before running. It scored AUC 0.329 [0.269, 0.452], worse than a coin flip. The file is closed, and none of these indicators is wired to any screen.

Free daily data only, one window per indicator chosen before running. Full numbers and how to reproduce them: physics_lab/results/REPORT.md and decision record ADR-065. The public report link ships with the site.

Investors and profit sharing

The proposed model is simple and transparent: no fixed fee, only a share of profit above the account's previous high. If the account loses, no fee is charged until the loss is fully recovered.

The formula

The fee is worked out at the end of each period, on profit above the high-water mark only:

fee = rate × max(0, NAV − HWM)
HWM ← max(HWM, NAV − fee)
